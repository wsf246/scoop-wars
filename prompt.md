You are playing Scoop Wars. A clue describes one news story published in the last few hours, without the names or words in its headline. Under the clue are the results of two news searches made with it: `any_language`, and `chinese_or_indonesian` (only articles in those languages). Answer with the URL of one article from those results that reports the clue's story, or with an empty url.

About one clue in five is a FAKE: a real story from today with one fact changed (another number, country, city, date or time of day, the opposite direction or reaction, a swapped cause, person or group). A fake still matches a real article on everything else, so a close match is not enough: one changed fact makes it a fake. A link for a fake loses 2 points and a blank wins 2. For a real clue a wrong link loses 2 and a blank loses nothing, so never guess.

Fill the answer in this order.

`story`: one sentence in English (at most 35 words) saying what the closest story in the results reports, with the real names and with ITS OWN numbers, places, dates, times and outcomes, taken from the articles and not from the clue.

`checks`: one entry for each concrete detail of the clue: every number, amount, count, age, score, date, weekday and time of day; every place and nationality; who did what to whom; the cause or reason; every direction (ahead or behind, rose or fell, from which into which); every reaction (praised or questioned). In each entry, `clue` is the detail as the clue states it, in a few words; `articles` is what the articles about that story state for it, in a few words copied from them, or "not stated" when none mentions it; `same` is false only when the articles state it differently. These are NOT differences: a paraphrase or general description that fits ("a G7 economy" for the UK, "a search giant" for Google), a rounded number ("about 800 billion" for 808 billion, "over a quarter-million" for 252,000), a detail that no article mentions. When two stories in the results look alike, the closest story is the one that agrees with the clue's details. When articles about the story disagree with each other on a detail, the clue agrees if one of them does: choose that article.

`url`: empty when any check has `same` false (the clue is a fake, or the story is another event of the same kind), and empty when no article clearly reports the story. Otherwise the url of one article that reports it:
- Language. If the story happens in mainland China, Hong Kong, Macau, Taiwan, Singapore or Malaysia, answer with a Chinese-language article. If it happens in Indonesia, answer with an Indonesian-language article. These must come from `chinese_or_indonesian`; use another language only when no article there reports the story. For stories anywhere else, answer with an English article when one reports it.
- Which article. Among the articles that report the story in the right language, choose the one with the earliest `published` time. Never choose an article published more than 24 hours before `now`. Copy the url exactly.
