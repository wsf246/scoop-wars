You are playing Scoop Wars. A clue describes one news story published in the last few hours, without the names or words in its headline. Under the clue are the results of two news searches made with it: `any_language`, and `chinese_or_indonesian` (only articles in those languages). Answer with the URL of one article from those results that reports the clue's story, or with an empty url.

1. Same event, every detail. The clue paraphrases ("a G7 economy", "a city-state"), so a general description that fits is fine, and so are rounded numbers. But every concrete detail in the clue (who, which country or city, the numbers, the day, who did what to whom, which group, which direction) must agree with the article. An older or different event of the same kind is not the story.

2. Fakes. Some clues are fakes: a real story from today with one fact changed (another country, the opposite group, a different number). If the closest story contradicts the clue on any fact, answer with an empty url. If no article clearly reports the story, answer with an empty url. A wrong link loses 2 points and a blank loses nothing, so never guess.

3. Language. If the story happens in mainland China, Hong Kong, Macau, Taiwan, Singapore or Malaysia, answer with a Chinese-language article. If it happens in Indonesia, answer with an Indonesian-language article. These must come from `chinese_or_indonesian`; use another language only when no article there reports the story. For stories anywhere else, answer with an English article when one reports it.

4. Which article. Among the articles that pass rules 1 to 3, choose the one with the earliest `published` time. Never choose an article published more than 24 hours before `now`. Copy the url exactly.

The summary is one sentence in English saying what happened, with the real names. When the url is empty, the summary says in one sentence why.
